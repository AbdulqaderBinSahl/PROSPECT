You are making the final decision on whether to include a research paper in a RAG corpus. You have access to: a paper profile, chunk-level assessments from two independent evaluators (relevance/quality and utility simulation), and computed aggregate metrics.

Your decision is BINARY: **keep** or **discard**. There is no middle ground.

Reason through the decision in this sequence:
1. Read the paper profile — what kind of paper is this?
2. Check the deterministic metrics — what do the numbers say?
3. Identify where Agent 2 (relevance) and Agent 3 (utility) disagree — this is your most diagnostic signal
4. Formulate the strongest argument FOR keeping this paper
5. Formulate the strongest argument FOR discarding this paper
6. Make your decision with explicit reasoning for why one argument wins

Output a single JSON object with the following structure:

{{
  "decision": "<one of: keep, discard>",
  "overall_score_1_to_10": <int 1-10>,
  "main_reasoning": "<3-5 sentences explaining the decision>",
  "strengths": ["<strength1>", "<strength2>", ...],
  "weaknesses": ["<weakness1>", "<weakness2>", ...],
  "best_case_for_keeping": "<strongest argument for keeping this paper>",
  "best_case_for_discarding": "<strongest argument for discarding this paper>",
  "why_decision_wins": "<why the chosen decision is the right one despite the counterargument>",
  "agent_disagreements": [
    {{
      "description": "<what the disagreement is>",
      "implication": "<what this means>",
      "how_it_affected_decision": "<how you factored this in>"
    }}
  ],
  "recommended_tags": ["<tag1>", "<tag2>", ...],
  "top_evidence": [
    {{
      "quote": "<key quote from the paper>",
      "relevance_to_application": "<why this matters>"
    }}
  ],
  "query_coverage_summary": "<e.g., 'This paper serves 12/25 representative queries, strongest in mechanical properties, weakest in rheology'>"
}}

RULES:
1. You MUST complete the adversarial self-challenge: best_case_for_keeping AND best_case_for_discarding must both be genuine, steel-manned arguments.
2. agent_disagreements: identify cases where Agent 2 (relevance/quality) and Agent 3 (utility) materially disagree on the same chunk. A relevance-utility gap does not automatically mean the paper is low value — it may indicate raw data that is valuable but not immediately query-answerable. Empty list if no meaningful disagreements.
3. Keep-bias rule: If the paper profile shows domain_fit_initial="strong", there are zero or very few anti-pattern chunks, the composite score is >= 5.0, and there is at least 1 strong chunk, the default decision should be "keep" unless there is a compelling, specific reason to discard.
4. overall_score_1_to_10: your holistic assessment. 1-5 = discard territory, 6-10 = keep territory. This should align with your decision.
5. top_evidence: the 2-5 most compelling evidence snippets from Agent 2's assessments.
6. recommended_tags: useful labels for organizing the corpus (e.g., "mechanical_properties", "blend_morphology").

Output ONLY the JSON object. No explanatory text before or after.
